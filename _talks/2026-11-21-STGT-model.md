This is a recording of a talk I gave at the Sign- and Goal-Tracking workshop organised by Prof. Daniel Schad in November 2026.
Abstract: Confronted with a common Pavlovian conditioning experience in which a lever predicts delivery of a reward, individual animals adopt strikingly distinct behaviours, known as sign-tracking, if they engage the predictive lever, and goal-tracking, if they prefer attending to the site of future reward delivery. In this talk, I will present a unifying computational framework which combines model-based decision-making, that relies on an internal representation of the task structure, with attribution of incentive salience to environmental stimuli. After describing the mechanisms of this model, I shall overview its success in fitting and explaining behavioural, physiological, and pharmacological data, before discussing predictions made by the model, in particular how to bias a group of animals towards goal-tracking, as well as implications for behaviour beyond Pavlovian conditioning.
